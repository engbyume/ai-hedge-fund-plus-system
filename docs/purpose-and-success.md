# Purpose and success criteria

## Personal objective

The system exists to test whether a customized, catalyst-aware portfolio can beat the S&P 500 without inheriting its fixed sector and security weights. The operator wants flexibility to keep a bullish posture when a dominant benchmark theme is weak, while accepting roughly benchmark performance when the market is strong.

The desired shape is high highs and high lows. That is an objective, not a promise. The minimum success condition is meaningful net benchmark outperformance during a defined review window, especially in a weak market. If that condition is not met by the declared failure review date, the operator should treat the experiment as failed or revise the hypothesis openly.

## Required declaration before measurement

Record these values before interpreting results:

- benchmark name and investable proxy
- start date and baseline account state
- return method
- treatment of deposits, withdrawals, dividends, taxes, fees, and cash
- portfolio risk budget and sector or position caps
- rebalance rule and trade frictions
- evaluation window and failure review date
- whether the record is simulated, paper, or live

If any item is missing, label the result provisional. Do not backfill a favorable baseline after seeing outcomes.

## Why customization matters

Customization can reduce dependence on a single sector or theme, such as an AI-led concentration in the benchmark. It can also increase model risk, turnover, tax drag, execution risk, and the chance of false confidence. The evidence process must measure both the intended benefit and these costs.

## Non-goals

- guaranteeing returns
- predicting every market move
- replacing independent investment judgment
- hiding losing periods or rejected ideas
- using a public repository as a place to store account state
